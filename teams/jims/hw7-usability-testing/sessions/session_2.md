# SettleWise | User Test — Session 2

**Participant:** P2
**Moderator / note-taker:** Izum Adnan
**Goal:** Evaluate whether a first-time user understands SettleWise, can upload apartment listings, and can compare apartments based on their priorities.
**Session length:** About 20 minutes

## Summary

P2 was able to sign up, add listings, pick priorities and generate a comparison, but he was overwhelmed for most of the session. The amount of text and the number of options on screen made it hard for him to know what to do next, and he read SettleWise as an apartment marketplace until the very end. The product only made sense to him once the comparison loaded and he saw the net effective rent, which was his aha moment.

## Usability Tasks

### Task 1: First Impressions

**Prompt:** "Please open the SettleWise website. Without clicking anything yet, what do you think this product does?"

**Observed:**

- Did not understand the purpose of SettleWise from the landing page.
- Misread the product as a tool for finding an apartment (a listings marketplace or rent finder) rather than a tool for comparing apartments he already found.
- Said he felt overwhelmed: "there was so much going on" on the page.
- Did not pick out the main value proposition (side-by-side comparison based on personal priorities).

**Notes:** The first screen is doing too much for a new visitor. P2's eye had no single place to land, and because nothing told him otherwise, he defaulted to the most familiar apartment-site pattern: search for listings.

### Task 2: Sign Up

**Prompt:** "Imagine you're looking for an apartment and are interested in trying this service. Please try to sign up."

**Result:** Completed

**Observed:**

- Found and completed signup without trouble.
- Skipped the tutorial entirely and did not go back to it.
- Struggled to navigate the site once he was past sign-in. He did not know where to go first or what the next step was.

**Notes:** Signup itself is not the problem. Skipping the tutorial left P2 with no mental model of the flow (find listings on StreetEasy, add them with the button, set priorities, compare), and the post-login screens did not give him one either. Users who skip the tutorial need the next step to be obvious from the page itself.

### Task 3: Upload Three Apartment Listings

**Prompt:** "Imagine you're looking for an apartment in New York City. Go to StreetEasy, find three apartments you would consider living in, and add all three listings to SettleWise."

**Result:** Partial

**Observed:**

- Had a hard time finding the button.
- Once he found it, did not understand how to use it.
- Expected the listing to be added as soon as he clicked the button, with no further steps.
- Once he had added a listing successfully, said the process was easy.

**Notes:** The difficulty is discovery and setup, not the action itself. After the first successful add, repeating it was easy. This points to fixing the first-time experience of the button (where it is, what it does, and confirming the add in one click) rather than redesigning the whole add flow.

### Task 4: Compare Apartments

**Prompt:** "Now imagine you're deciding which of these apartments is the best fit for you. Select at least three priorities that matter most to you, then use SettleWise to generate a comparison of your three apartments."

**Result:** Completed (with heavy confusion)

**Priorities selected:** 3+

**Aha moment:** Yes

**Observed:**

- Extremely overwhelmed. Did not know how to navigate the website and said there was too much going on and he did not know what to follow.
- Clicked the Compare Apartments tab and stayed confused the whole way through, up to and including choosing his priorities.
- Expected the comparison to appear right after clicking, but there was another page to navigate before getting into the comparison.
- It only clicked for him after he pressed Done on his priorities and the app compared the apartments for him.
- Reached the aha moment when he saw the net effective rent on the comparison.

**Notes:** The comparison output is the strongest part of the product for P2; the path to get there is the weakest. Every screen between "Compare Apartments" and the results added confusion. Net effective rent was the specific detail that made the value clear, so it should be surfaced early and prominently.

## Interview

**1. When did you first understand what this product is for?**

Only at the very end. P2 thought SettleWise was a type of marketplace for apartments at first, and did not realize it was a comparison tool until the comparison was generated.

**2. What was the most confusing part?**

The amount of text. He said it was not that the site wasn't nice looking, but that there was too much on screen and too much going on, so he did not know what to do. The button and the apartment comparison were the most confusing parts.

**3. What would have made it easier?**

A simpler path with less going on. He felt that too many things were left to interpretation, and a more direct, step-by-step flow would have made it much easier to understand.

**4. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**

Yes, if he knew what it was for from the start.

**5. "If you could wave a magic wand and change something about the app, what would it be?"**

Less text on the screen and a simpler experience overall.

### Key Observations

**1. Where did the participant get stuck?**

- Finding and using the button to add listings from StreetEasy.
- The Start Comparison / Compare Apartments flow, from clicking the tab through choosing priorities.
- General navigation after sign-in, made worse by skipping the tutorial.

**2. What unexpected behaviors did you observe?**

- Read SettleWise as an apartment marketplace or rent finder from the landing page, and held that belief until the end.
- Skipped the tutorial entirely.
- Expected one click to do the job: the button to add a listing immediately, and the comparison to appear immediately after clicking.
- Visible overwhelm throughout, driven by the volume of text on each screen.

**3. What should we change, and why?**

- Make the homepage direct about what SettleWise does. It currently looks like a rent finder. A short headline that says it compares apartments you have already found, based on your priorities, would prevent the marketplace misread.
- Cut text and simplify every screen. Give each page one clear next action so users are not left to interpret what to follow.
- Make the button easier to find and use. Ideally one click adds the listing and confirms it was added.
- Shorten the path to the comparison. Remove the extra page between choosing priorities and seeing results.
- Lead with net effective rent. It was the moment the product made sense, so surface it earlier.
- Add a profile page so users have a clear home base after signing in.

### Biggest Takeaway

P2 was overwhelmed by the amount of text and the number of things happening on each screen. The core comparison works and delivered a clear aha moment, but users need a simpler, more direct path to reach it. He also thought the name sounded too similar to Splitwise.
