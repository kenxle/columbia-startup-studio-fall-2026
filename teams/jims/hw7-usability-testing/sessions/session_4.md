# SettleWise | User Testing — Session 4

**Session date:** November 6, 2026
**Participant:** P4
**Note-taker:** Samantha Qian

**Goal:** Evaluate whether first-time users understand SettleWise, can upload apartment listings, and can compare apartments based on their priorities.

**Session length:** Approximately 20 minutes

## 1. Introduction & Consent

Thanked the participant, reminded them to think out loud, that we won't help during tasks, and that there are no wrong answers. Explained that notes may be shared anonymously with the class and that they can stop at any time. Asked permission to record the screen and the session.

## 2. Usability Tasks

### Task 1 — First Impressions

**Say:** "Please open the SettleWise website. Without clicking anything yet, what do you think this product does?"

**Notes:** Understood this site is used for apartment searching. Scrolled through the home page. Stated apartment animations are nice.

### Task 2 — Sign Up

**Say:** "Imagine you're looking for an apartment and are interested in trying this service. Please try to sign up."

**Notes:** Executed really fast. (Might have been rushing due to time constraint of user test.)

### Task 3 — Upload Three Apartment Listings

**Say:** "Imagine you're looking for an apartment in New York City. Go to StreetEasy, find three apartments you would consider living in, and add all three listings to SettleWise."

**Notes:** Struggled with the button. At first they were dragging the button to the tabs. Didn't bother to read the instructions. Trying and failing to add apartments. Navigated to comparison page and found no apartments added. "Drop it to bookmarks? How do I do that?" Then gave up and slowed down to read the instructions. Looked up StreetEasy. Set budget 1500. Selected Manhattan for location. Noted how few apartment options there were.

### Task 4 — Compare Apartments

**Say:** "Now imagine you're deciding which of these apartments is the best fit for you. Select at least three priorities that matter most to you, then use SettleWise to generate a comparison of your three apartments."

**Notes:** Quickly established laundry and bathrooms as priorities. Continued to select other options as priorities. Generated comparison but it didn't seem like they reached the aha moment. Red note under listing to the very left.

## 3. Interview the User

**1. When did you first understand what this product is for?**

When they saw the home page, didn't understand how it really worked until tutorial.

**2. What was the most confusing part?**

Adding the button to bookmarks bar. More intuitive if it was an extension. Some people don't want to move around bookmarks.

**3. What would have made it easier?**

Extension.

**4. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**

Didn't ask this question.

**5. "If you could wave a magic wand and change something about the app, what would it be?"**

Fetch more details. Right now it's limited to existing fields. Want description of what people who have lived in the property have said. Want a review. Fills in missing info gaps.

## 4. Post-Test Researcher Summary

**1. Where did the participant get stuck?**

Adding the button and finding the intuitive order of going from adding apartments to generating a comparison.

**2. What unexpected behaviors did you observe?**

User didn't seem to care at all about what was generated. Seemed like they knew what to expect.

**3. What should we change, and why?**

Aha moment can perhaps include AI-generated insight to provide a recommendation on housing. Or AI can be used to connect some dots for the user based on their priorities. We still aren't able to draw many conclusions from looking at a table.

**4. Biggest takeaway from this session:**

Questioning the helpfulness of the aha moment.

## Moderator Reminders

- Don't guide the participant. Let them figure things out independently.
- Ask neutral questions only. If stuck, ask: "What are you looking for?"
- Record behavior, not judgments. Write "Clicked signup twice" instead of "Signup was confusing."
- Encourage thinking aloud, without suggesting what they should do.
- Watch for the aha moment. Did they understand why comparing apartments through SettleWise is useful?
- Respect consent. Don't record without permission or include identifying details in shared findings.
- Paint a picture for them. Understand their motivation for adding these houses, not just random houses.
