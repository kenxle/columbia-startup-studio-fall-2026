# SettleWise | User Testing — Session 1

**Session date:** November 5, 2026
**Participant:** Participant 1 (P1)
**Note-taker:** Maria Aezah

**Goal:** See whether a first-time user understands SettleWise, can save three apartment listings, and can compare them based on what matters to them.

**Session length:** About 24 minutes

## 1. Introduction & Consent

Participation: Yes. Screen recording: Yes. Voice transcription: Yes. Anonymous class findings explained: Yes. Right to stop at any time: Not confirmed in the transcript. Social media permission: Not documented.

We explained that we were testing the website, not the participant, and asked her to think out loud. We also told her we would take notes and share findings anonymously.

## 2. Usability Tasks

### Task 1: First Impressions

**Result:** Partial understanding

At first, she thought SettleWise was a place to find or save apartments. She did not understand that comparing apartments was the main point. She moved forward without really knowing what the website would help her do.

### Task 2: Sign Up

**Result:** Completed

She signed up and got to the tutorial, but skipped it without really understanding it. She was able to create a search group and continue. Signing up was not the issue; understanding what to do after signing up was.

### Task 3: Add Three Apartment Listings

**Result:** Completed | Listings added: 3 of 3

She searched StreetEasy and added three apartments. She looked in the Bronx and mentioned affordability. The hardest part was the bookmark tool. She saved it under All Bookmarks instead of the bookmarks bar, so each time she wanted to add a listing, she had to go through her bookmarks. She still found a way to add all three apartments.

**What we helped with:** We explained StreetEasy and clarified the task while she searched. We showed her the easier way to put the bookmark on the bookmarks bar only after she finished adding the listings. She said that way was easier.

### Task 4: Compare Apartments

**Result:** Completed | Aha moment: Yes | Exact final priority selection: Not verified

She generated the comparison herself. This is where she finally understood why SettleWise was useful. But the Edit Comparison Fields page was confusing. She did not know what non-negotiables meant or what the priority fields were asking her to do. When she saw that 3 out of 3 listings had bedroom or bathroom information, she thought it meant three rooms, rather than three listings containing that information.

She also did not seem to understand how changing the order of priorities affected the comparison. The feedback that stood out to her was when SettleWise showed that an apartment did not meet her rent requirement. That made the result more meaningful.

**What we helped with:** We repeated or clarified some task instructions. The more detailed explanation of non-negotiables and field counts came after she had already generated the comparison.

## 3. Interview the User

**1. When did you first understand what this product is for?**

She understood it when she reached the comparison. Before that, she thought she was just collecting apartment listings. The comparison helped her see that SettleWise was meant to help her choose between them.

**2. What was the most confusing part?**

She did not point to one single problem at first, but we saw confusion with the bookmark tool and the Edit Comparison Fields section. Non-negotiables and the numbers next to the fields were not clear.

**3. What would have made it easier?**

Clearer steps for setting up the bookmark tool, and a simpler comparison setup. After seeing the bookmark on the bookmarks bar, she said it was easier than opening All Bookmarks every time.

**4. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**

She said she would use it if she were looking for an apartment. Comparing listings in one place was useful, although she normally searches on her phone and was less familiar with this computer-based process.

**5. If you could change one thing, what would it be?**

She wanted the comparison settings broken into smaller steps, especially separating non-negotiables from the other fields. A Next button would give users a chance to understand one section before moving on.

## 4. Post-Test Researcher Summary

| Evaluation | Result |
|---|---|
| Understood product purpose | Partial at first; yes by comparison |
| Completed signup | Yes |
| Added 3 listings | Yes |
| Selected 3+ priorities | Not fully verified |
| Generated comparison page | Yes, independently |
| Reached aha moment | Yes |

**1. Where did the participant get stuck?**

The website's purpose was not clear at first. She skipped the tutorial, struggled to set up the bookmark, and had trouble understanding the comparison fields, especially non-negotiables and the 3 out of 3 labels.

**2. What unexpected behaviors did you observe?**

She kept moving through the website even when she did not understand parts of it. Instead of using the bookmark bar, she worked through All Bookmarks. She also clicked around in the comparison settings while misunderstanding what some of the numbers meant. Finishing a task did not always mean she understood it.

**3. What should we change, and why?**

We need to explain what SettleWise does much earlier. The tutorial should make the next steps clearer, especially how to set up the bookmark. The comparison settings should be split into smaller sections, with plain explanations of non-negotiables, priorities, and what the numbers beside the fields mean. The rent feedback was helpful, so we should make this kind of feedback easy to notice.

**4. Biggest takeaway from this session**

She finished all the main tasks, but we were still giving her the next task to do. Without that direction, I think it would have taken her much longer to figure out what came next. She might have lost interest before reaching the comparison, which is the part that made the website useful to her. We need the website itself to guide users better, without depending on someone telling them the next step.

## 5. Note for Future Testing

Next time, we should give people a reason to care about the apartments they choose. Instead of asking them to add any three listings, we can give them a realistic situation, like moving to New York City with a budget and a few things they really need. Then we can see whether SettleWise actually helps them choose, rather than just whether they can complete the steps.

**Suggested updated task wording**

> Imagine you are moving to New York City soon and need an apartment within your budget. Think about what matters most to you, like rent, location, bedrooms, or laundry. Find three apartments you would seriously consider living in, add them to SettleWise, and use the comparison to decide which one fits you best.

**Researcher note:** The task instructions and clarifications helped the participant move through the website. We should not treat every completed task as proof that she would have found the same steps on her own.
