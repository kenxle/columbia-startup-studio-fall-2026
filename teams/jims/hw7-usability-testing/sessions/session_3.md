# SettleWise | User Test — Session 3

**Participant:** P3
**Moderator / note-taker:** Izum Adnan
**Goal:** Evaluate whether a first-time user understands SettleWise, can upload apartment listings, and can compare apartments based on their priorities.
**Session length:** About 20 minutes

## Summary

P3 had a reasonable idea of what SettleWise does from the start and liked the UI, and the tutorial confirmed his understanding. His main frustration was switching back and forth between SettleWise and StreetEasy, which made him visibly annoyed, especially after the button took him a long time to figure out. He completed signup, selected priorities and generated a comparison, but he navigated the comparison flow mostly blind and did not reach the aha moment.

## Usability Tasks

### Task 1: First Impressions

**Prompt:** "Please open the SettleWise website. Without clicking anything yet, what do you think this product does?"

**Observed:**

- Liked the UI.
- Thought SettleWise would help him find and compare apartments.
- Mostly understood the purpose: he picked up on comparison, though he also expected help finding apartments.

**Notes:** The landing page communicated comparison to P3, which is closer to the real purpose than other participants got. The "find" part of his expectation foreshadows his later wish that listings were already inside SettleWise.

### Task 2: Sign Up

**Prompt:** "Imagine you're looking for an apartment and are interested in trying this service. Please try to sign up."

**Result:** Completed

**Observed:**

- Signed up and completed the tutorial.
- Wished there was a video tutorial instead of (or alongside) the current one.
- Wished SettleWise was already present on StreetEasy so he did not have to navigate between the two websites.

**Notes:** P3 engaged with onboarding, but the written tutorial did not fully prepare him for the button step that followed. His request for video came up here first and kept coming up for the rest of the session.

### Task 3: Upload Three Apartment Listings

**Prompt:** "Imagine you're looking for an apartment in New York City. Go to StreetEasy, find three apartments you would consider living in, and add all three listings to SettleWise."

**Result:** Partial

**Observed:**

- Struggled a lot with the button and did not know what to do with it.
- Repeated several times that he wanted a video-style tutorial showing how the button works.
- Became visibly annoyed at having to keep switching between SettleWise and StreetEasy.
- Expected that once he pressed the button, it would do the work for him.

**Notes:** This was the low point of the session. Even after completing the tutorial, P3 could not work out the button, and the repeated tab switching turned confusion into frustration. A short demo of the button in action would have directly answered what he kept asking for.

### Task 4: Compare Apartments

**Prompt:** "Now imagine you're deciding which of these apartments is the best fit for you. Select at least three priorities that matter most to you, then use SettleWise to generate a comparison of your three apartments."

**Result:** Completed

**Priorities selected:** 3+

**Aha moment:** No

**Observed:**

- Navigated the website and somewhat knew what was happening, but did not really understand the gist of it.
- Was navigating blindly until he reached the priorities step.
- Once he chose his priorities, he was fine for the rest of the task.
- Expected the comparison to appear right after clicking, but there was another page to navigate before getting into the comparison.

**Notes:** P3 got through the flow but never felt in control of it. The priorities screen was the first point where the purpose of each step was clear to him. Too many clicks are needed to get from listings to a comparison.

## Interview

**1. When did you first understand what this product is for?**

He had a rough idea from the start, and it became solid while he was doing the tutorial.

**2. What was the most confusing part?**

Navigating back and forth between SettleWise and StreetEasy, especially after it took him so long to add the button. He thought that once he pressed the button, it would work for him.

**3. What would have made it easier?**

Not having to go back and forth between SettleWise and StreetEasy.

**4. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**

Yes, if he knew what it was for from the start.

**5. "If you could wave a magic wand and change something about the app, what would it be?"**

Not having to navigate back and forth from StreetEasy, and having all the listings already in SettleWise for him.

### Key Observations

**1. Where did the participant get stuck?**

- The button: finding out what it does and how to use it, even after completing the tutorial.
- The Start Comparison page, where he navigated without understanding the gist until he reached priorities.

**2. What unexpected behaviors did you observe?**

- Repeatedly asked for a video tutorial during the task, not only in the interview.
- Visible annoyance from moving back and forth between SettleWise and StreetEasy.
- Expected the button to do the work as soon as he pressed it, and the comparison to appear right after clicking.

**3. What should we change, and why?**

- Add a short video tutorial, especially one that shows the button being used on a StreetEasy listing. P3 asked for this several times.
- Make the button easier to use. It took him a long time, and it should work in one press.
- Reduce the clicks needed to reach the comparison. The UI feels like a series of buttons before getting to the goal of comparing apartments.
- Make it easier to reach StreetEasy listings from SettleWise, for example a link to StreetEasy on the site or a mini web page that opens inside SettleWise, so users are not switching tabs.

### Biggest Takeaway

P3 was visibly annoyed by having to navigate between SettleWise and StreetEasy. He understood the idea and liked the UI, but the switching between websites and the hard-to-learn button kept him from seeing the value of the comparison.
