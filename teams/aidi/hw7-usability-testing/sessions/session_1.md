### Task-by-task observations

#### Task 1: Explore the public pages and join the waitlist

- **What they did:** Explored the homepage and landing pages, compared how the pages appeared on different devices, and found the waitlist entry point and form without assistance.
- **What they said out loud:** The page structure communicated the product clearly, but some layouts felt too empty. The core value proposition should be more prominent, and the real-interview feature should appear earlier. The waitlist was easy to find, but what happens after joining was unclear.
- **Completed?** Yes

#### Task 2: Enter preferences, search, and filter colleges

- **What they did:** Entered background information and preferences, searched for colleges, and reviewed the available filters. They paused after completing fields to look for a save or next-step action.
- **What they said out loud:** Campus setting, undergraduate population, student-faculty ratio, admissions policies, need-based aid, scholarships, and several filter choices needed explanations. They were unsure whether some fields were optional and whether filters applied immediately. Some English terms remained in the Chinese interface.
- **Completed?** With trouble

#### Task 3 (aha moment): Review a college detail page and verify its sources

- **What they did:** Opened a college detail page, scrolled through the page to find specific information, and located the original data-source links.
- **What they said out loud:** The source links made the information feel more credible. The page needed faster navigation, clearer location and cost information, explanations for admissions and crime categories, and a table of contents or sticky navigation.
- **Completed?** With trouble

**Hesitations:** Paused at unfamiliar preference and filter terms, after completing the preference fields, and while searching the long detail page for specific sections.

**Wrong clicks:** None observed.

**Questions out loud:** Asked whether preference fields were optional, whether the information had been saved, what several specialized terms meant, and what was included in the displayed cost.

**Dropped tasks:** None.

**Workarounds:** Scrolled through the full detail page because there was no table of contents or section navigation.

**Did they reach the aha moment?** Yes. They reached it when they noticed the original data-source links and said that being able to verify the information increased its credibility.

### Post-session answers

1. **Overall impression:** The interface was intuitive, concise, smooth, and credible.
2. **Most confusing or difficult part:** Specialized terminology, unclear save and next-step behavior, mixed-language labels, and navigation within the detail page.
3. **Most important improvement:** Add clear explanations for specialized terms and provide an explicit save and next-step action.

### Quick insight

The biggest thing this session showed us was that even a participant who works in college admissions counseling needed clearer explanations for specialized options and metrics.
