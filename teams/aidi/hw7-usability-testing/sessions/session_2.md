### Task-by-task observations

#### Task 1: Explore the public pages and join the waitlist

- **What they did:** Explored the homepage and landing pages and completed the waitlist form without assistance.
- **What they said out loud:** The product purpose was understandable, but the top navigation was unclear and the homepage looked like an early-stage startup. Some claims sounded too absolute. The landing pages explained individual features, but did not fully explain how college fit would be determined. The waitlist button and form were clear.
- **Completed?** Yes

#### Task 2: Enter preferences, search, and filter colleges

- **What they did:** Entered background information and preferences, searched for colleges, and applied filters. They paused when changes in city selection were not visually obvious and after completing the preference fields to look for a save or next-step action.
- **What they said out loud:** Several terms, student-population metrics, student-faculty ratio, scholarship concepts, and admissions-policy choices needed explanations. The admissions-policy selection logic felt reversed. The search bar and filter logic were otherwise clear.
- **Completed?** With trouble

#### Task 3 (aha moment): Review a college detail page and verify its sources

- **What they did:** Opened a college detail page, reviewed the information, located the original sources, and noticed the verification date.
- **What they said out loud:** The detail page was clear and trustworthy, and the source links increased confidence in the information. Crime categories could be sorted, ranking methodology should be explained, and more lifestyle information would be useful.
- **Completed?** Yes

**Hesitations:** Paused at unfamiliar preference terms and metrics, when the selected city did not visibly update, and after completing the preference fields without a clear save or next-step action.

**Wrong clicks:** None observed.

**Questions out loud:** Asked what several specialized terms and scholarship options meant, how college fit was determined, and how rankings were calculated.

**Dropped tasks:** None.

**Workarounds:** None observed.

**Did they reach the aha moment?** Yes. They reached it when they located the original sources and verification date and said that these details made the information more trustworthy.

### Post-session answers

1. **Overall impression:** The search, filters, and institution details were clear, detailed, and trustworthy.
2. **Most confusing or difficult part:** Specialized terms, preference logic, unclear save and next-step behavior, and the explanation of college fit.
3. **Most important improvement:** Explain specialized options and methodologies and make the completion state of the preference flow explicit.

### Quick insight

The biggest thing this session showed us was that a participant who had already completed the college-application process still needed explanations for specialized options and classifications.
