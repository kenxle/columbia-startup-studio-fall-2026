# Usability Testing Synthesis

- **Sessions run:** 3
- **Participants who reached the aha moment:** 3 out of 3

## Aha moment

The aha moment occurred when participants reached an institution detail page, noticed the original data-source links, and recognized that the information was verifiable and trustworthy. All three participants said that showing the sources increased the product's credibility.

## Patterns across sessions

- **Unclear specialized terminology (3 of 3 sessions):** All three participants paused at unfamiliar options, metrics, financial-aid concepts, admissions policies, or other specialized classifications.
- **Unclear save and next-step behavior (3 of 3 sessions):** All three participants looked for a clear way to save or continue after entering background information and preferences.
- **Incomplete Chinese localization (2 of 3 sessions):** P1 and P3 encountered unexplained English content while using the Chinese interface.
- **Slow navigation within institution details (2 of 3 sessions):** P1 and P3 had to scroll through long pages because there was no table of contents or section navigation.
- **Source transparency increased trust (3 of 3 sessions):** Every participant reacted positively to the original data-source links and said they made the information more credible.

## What surprised us

We were surprised that even experienced participants did not understand some specialized options and classifications. One participant works in college admissions counseling, and another had previously completed the college-application process. Their confusion showed that domain experience alone is not enough; specialized terms and categories still require clear explanations and examples.

## What we're changing and why

> - **What we observed:** All three participants could not tell whether their background and preference information had been saved or what to do next.
> - **What we're changing:** Add a clear **Save and Continue** action, a visible saved state, and a completion message.
> - **Why:** The uncertainty appeared in every session and interrupted the core onboarding flow.

> - **What we observed:** All three participants needed help understanding specialized terms, metrics, and options.
> - **What we're changing:** Add bilingual explanations, examples, and reference ranges for specialized terminology and data.
> - **Why:** Participants with different levels of admissions experience could not make informed choices from the labels alone.

> - **What we observed:** P1 and P3 encountered English content while using the Chinese interface.
> - **What we're changing:** Complete the Chinese localization. Keep an English term only when an exact translation is unsuitable, and provide a Chinese explanation with it.
> - **Why:** Mixed-language content created avoidable confusion in a product designed for bilingual use.

> - **What we observed:** P1 and P3 could not move quickly between sections of the institution detail page.
> - **What we're changing:** Add a persistent table of contents or side navigation and a clear way to return to the search results.
> - **Why:** Faster navigation will help users locate and compare specific information without scanning the entire page.

> - **What we observed:** The waitlist entry point was discoverable, but the post-submission state was not always clear.
> - **What we're changing:** Show a clear confirmation and next steps after submission, and keep the waitlist entry accessible from the top navigation.
> - **Why:** Users should know whether their submission succeeded and what will happen next.

## What we're not changing (yet)

- We are not yet expanding the product with additional lifestyle-cost, transportation, campus-employment, research, and broader campus-life information. These ideas are useful, but they do not block the current core flow.
- We are not yet redesigning the overall homepage visual style or tone. We will first address the interaction problems that directly affected task completion and comprehension.
