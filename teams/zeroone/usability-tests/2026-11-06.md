# CallIt usability findings, November 6, 2026

CallIt lets private groups make predictions and see who called them correctly. We tested it with three people using it for the first time: P4, P5 and P6. Below, we describe what they did, what they told us and what we propose changing.

## Study coverage

- Recruitment plan: six slots. We have records for three people who attended and completed a session. All three are included here. Bookings and attendance for the other slots are unconfirmed.
- Participants: none had used CallIt before. We found them through a personal network, an apartment group chat and a shared apartment. These contacts were easy for us to reach, so the sample may differ from people who discover CallIt on their own.
- Devices: two desktop Chrome sessions and one Android Edge session, all dated November 6 on the production app. Build identifiers were not recorded.
- Consent and recording: moderators report that all three agreed to participate, be recorded and have anonymous observations used in class. All three sessions were recorded. Recordings and consent records remain in restricted team storage. This report is based on the session notes.
- Coverage: all three attempted joining, creating a suggestion, finding it after manager publication and answering another call. P4 used a sports template; P5 wrote a custom question. P6 needed help with required fields. All three skipped the optional resolved-call task.
- Assistance and limits: P4 asked for confirmation before joining and needed the creation task clarified. P5 was told that interest selection could be skipped. P6 needed help finding required fields and understanding the waiting state. Signup was partly off-record for privacy. P6's signup row describes group creation, so it does not tell us whether signup needed help. Task durations differ from timestamps within a recording.

## Write-up for the class

We completed three sessions with people unfamiliar with CallIt, using two computers and one Android phone. Participants joined a group, created a prediction suggestion, found it after manager publication and answered another call. The clearest shared difficulty was recognizing whether a suggestion was already published. One participant inferred the waiting state after reading the screen, one described the workflow without clearly identifying the current state, and one needed an explanation.

Our first proposed change is a clearer message after submission: the suggestion is waiting for a manager, who can see it, and where to find it again. We also want to test a clearer creation button and more visible required fields. These address P4's confusion between creating and answering, P5's request for a more visible button, and P6's difficulty finding required fields. Answering was easier: all three completed that task and explained whose picks were visible.

We tested a small group recruited through personal or residential contacts, and helped with some tasks. We did not test completed results or whether people returned later. We will need further sessions to see whether people can use the revised labels without help and explain whether their suggestion is waiting or published.
